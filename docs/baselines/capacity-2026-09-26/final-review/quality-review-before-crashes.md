## Remaining practical risks, in priority order

1. **Qualify large crash recovery before recommending an operating rate.** `worker/mod.rs:478` hydrates all borrowed records and authorizes them before recovery dispatch; `send.rs:277` similarly processes recycled work beyond the new-allocation limit. Concurrency is bounded, total cycle latency is not. Slow/unknown calls can delay confirmation and other wallet work. Current small controlled tests and nominal drain results do not establish high-backlog recovery latency or fairness. Cuts 4/7/8 should preserve unresolved custody and distinguish a surviving 600-second lease from lost intent; they must not force-clear it.
2. **NOOP recovery is intentionally incomplete.** `gap_replay.rs` stops at a NOOP, and a rejected NOOP remains submitted/reserved. `send_tests.rs:400` verifies safe retention, not eventual recovery. `docs/design/finality-and-recovery.md` correctly says automated NOOP wire recovery is absent. This can stall that wallet until explicit reconciliation; do not promise automatic recovery of every nonce gap.
3. **Durability and retained-history capacity remain deployment obligations.** FULL sync and exact mirror checks have fault tests, not physical power-loss/storage certification. Independent ledger rollback/loss, clones or multiple hosts remain outside the supported topology. History grows durably; startup indexes fix lookup scans but do not supply retention or disk provisioning. Shared overload is measured; attribution to the global journal mutex/fsync still needs phase timing, not inference from a historical synthetic benchmark.
4. **Linux coverage of the new harness is pending.** The four CI gates passed at this SHA, including Rust/Redis/local-chain scenarios. `.github/workflows/ci-hardening.yaml` does not yet run the new capacity/resource/custody Python suites. Their 155 local checks and actual snapshot restores remain separately archived evidence; the pending workflow patch is not an executed Linux gate.
